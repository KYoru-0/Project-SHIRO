# Project-SHIRO
Shiro is a round fluff that listens to your music, vibe to it while being absolutely adorable on your Android phone

The editable body artwork is [artwork/shiro-body.svg](artwork/shiro-body.svg): a transparent, tail-free silhouette with ears and no face. Android uses the matching `shiro_body_neutral.xml` vector drawable; the eyes remain separate. Keep both paths in sync when editing. Jelly sprites and collision outlines are generated from this vector at runtime.

## Music stage

- While connecting to the playback service, startup shows a floating, blinking Shiro above a small glass plinth, a wordmark, and a five-bar loading indicator. It crossfades into the player as soon as the connection is ready, without an artificial delay or invented percentage. Connection failure offers Retry; a connection still pending after ten seconds offers Retry too. The mascot uses existing vector artwork and one lifecycle-aware animation, stopped when backgrounded, failed, or system animations are disabled. `ShiroLoadingScene.kt` includes normal, error, and large-text landscape previews for Android Studio. Ordinary track changes keep the player visible.
- **Menu → Visualizer** has two modes, **Spectrum** and **Liquid**, each with **Floor** and **Circle** layouts: four combinations. Choose both, then Apply; Cancel leaves the current combination unchanged. Both preferences are saved, and changing either preserves playback and the split/merged cat population. Halo, Resonance, Ripples, Orbits, and Glass playground are removed from the picker. Old Halo/Resonance preferences restore as Spectrum/Circle; other retired choices fall back to Spectrum/Floor.
- Spectrum/Floor keeps the 24 glass bars and slow peak trails along the song-details boundary. Spectrum/Circle wraps those same 24 bands around the artwork with real gaps and radial peak trails. Liquid/Floor keeps the three translucent ribbons. Liquid/Circle wraps a continuous, seamless wave around the artwork, with two inner decorative layers and local dents at cat contact. Drawing and collisions share the actual outer segment endpoints; peak trails and inner wave layers remain decorative.
- Both Circle combinations share the central physics: bass strengthens suction, Shiros circulate around the moving bar/wave edge, and strong beats release up to three nearby cats outward. Contact dents follow the actual deformed border; smaller cats respond more strongly. Dragging overrides the field, including through its center, and release recovers smoothly. Attraction eases in on playback and fades out on pause, silence, or sleep. Floor combinations continue to push from below.
- All four combinations share the existing audio analysis and bounded 120 Hz cat physics, with no extra decoder, blur pass, or rendering engine. Circular liquid uses 96 segments and each cat reuses its radial contact buffer. `app/build/reports/shiro-circular-performance.txt` records ten-cat circular simulations with pause/recovery checks; it is desktop CPU evidence, not Android frame timing. `shiro-circular-physics-preview.png` in the same directory is a geometry diagnostic, not an Android screenshot.
- Each spectrum bar represents one frequency band, from bass to treble. Floor bars run left to right. Both Circle modes rotate their ordered audio pattern around a fixed center, choosing a random starting angle per track and easing toward it on song changes. Energy adjusts the speed, with gradual direction changes every 16–28 seconds. Bars, peak trails, liquid dents and collision edges share the same rotation; contact velocity includes rotating slopes. Pause/silence decelerates the rotation, and changing circle style, seeking, or reopening the menu does not choose a new angle. The spectrum reads the playing track's decoded audio; it does not use the microphone.
- Embedded album art fills only the upper player area (menu, cats, and wave) at 28% opacity, center-cropped with no blur. The decoded image is retained at up to 1024 pixels on its longest edge instead of upscaling a blurred 256-pixel thumbnail. It ends exactly at the top edge of the song details. Decoding and palette extraction run off the main thread; the UI applies transparency only.
- The cover's dominant colored accent creates a coordinated theme for the song details, text, seek bar, favorite icon, menu button, and playback buttons. Light surfaces and dark accents keep labels readable, including on yellow artwork. Bars use a vertical gradient of lighter-to-deeper shades of the same hue rather than fading into black; monochrome art uses silver shades. The former curve and blue crest line are removed. Colors transition gently between tracks, and tracks without artwork return to blue.
- Five smaller jelly cats move independently across the wave. A deformable mesh makes their bellies bulge, bodies bend, and faces follow the wobble. Lighter gravity and springier impacts let the wave launch them higher. They roll around the Z axis, bump into each other, and stay inside the scene above the controls. Tap one to bounce just that cat. The wobble is damped so they eventually settle.
- Each Shiro has an invisible, firm circular core and a soft outer layer. The visible border dents toward the core at bar contact, with a small outward bulge around the dent. Core material stays fixed and outer material cannot cross it. Strong landings compress immediately, resting on a visible bar leaves a small loaded dent, and the shell springs back after release.
- Collision and touch outlines trace the opaque artwork and follow the same mesh and rotation as the drawing, preserving indentations instead of filling them with a convex outline. Cat-to-cat separation checks actual contours before applying its conservative separation response. The core is a simple deformation constraint, not a full soft-body simulation.
- Cats land on the visible bar tops, with exact outline intersections even at tiny overlaps and no invisible support across gaps. Impacts press upward at the contact point, using the bar width to set the deformation spread. The impression stays attached to the rotating body during its rebound, and the collision outline follows it. Taps and wall/cat collisions retain the general jelly wobble.
- Bar heights interpolate between audio updates. Pausing lowers them over roughly one second; resuming quickly raises them as audio data arrives. Song details span the screen with inset text and consistent spacing. Playback controls press down when touched. Short windows scroll to keep the cat and controls reachable.
- The artwork palette now styles the whole app: menu, playlist/search/favorites, Inputs, sleep timer, customization, and error dialogs share the player's colors. Glass panels use translucent tints, gentle gradients, light edge highlights, rounded corners, and restrained shadows on opaque surfaces. Translucent panels use tint and edge lighting without native elevation, avoiding an inner shadow patch behind text. Drawer/dialog backgrounds are opaque so artwork, cats, and text cannot show through; quieter glass cards sit on cached color gradients. The main artwork still ends at the flat top edge of the full-width song details. Styling adds no live backdrop blur, bitmap capture, or new animation loop. Destructive actions remain red and eye-color swatches retain their actual colors. Without artwork, the shared theme falls back to blue.
- Regular glass panels, including the song details, use 8–20% fill opacity. One page backdrop continues behind the details, controls and bottom padding; the lower section no longer restarts the gradient. Seeking and transport share a floating panel with a quieter 4–10% fill and balanced vertical edge lighting, avoiding a bright left strip. The full-width details have a stronger title/artist hierarchy, a track badge and a thin lit edge. Dense surfaces, including drawers/dialogs, use fully opaque tinted gradients with subtle highlights. The cover image stays above the details; only its palette continues below. Contrast checks include the stronger backdrop and both ends of the glass gradients.
- The artwork-colored overlay is restrained even during loud passages: each sheet is capped at 24% opacity (previously 80%), frequency-driven travel is halved, bends are smaller, and the white highlight is softer. The three cached mesh sheets still follow the selected visualizer and fade out on pause; the old ambient light pools stay removed. The unblurred translucent cover and static page tint remain. Hidden scenes retain the overlay pose without running its clock.
- Buttons, favorites, menu/playlist rows, filter chips and the customization switch share a brief glass press response: 4% compression, a slight downward movement and a brightened rim, releasing over 180ms. Native click/toggle, focus, ripple and disabled behavior remain in charge. The effect uses cached brushes and only animates during interaction; it follows Compose's system animation-duration scale. Primary buttons have a stationary upper reflection.
- The stage uses lightweight 2.5D materials: pearly Shiros with soft underside shading, curved highlights, and faint artwork-colored reflections; translucent spectrum columns with bright top edges and shaded sides; and glass droplets with a brief landing glint. Body lighting is baked into the three shared eye-state sprites, which still use one mesh draw per cat. Small projected shadows fade with height and stay inside bar faces. This adds no live blur, 3D engine, physics loop, or change to body silhouettes/collision geometry. Device smoothness still needs manual testing at the ten-cat limit.
- Live motion also follows RMS audio level, detected beat strength, and estimated tempo. Strong, consistent fast rhythms produce sharper bar rises, quicker recovery between beats, and stronger rebounds when cats actually land on visible bars. Quiet music stays gentler, steady tones do not generate rhythmic pulses, and uncertain tempo contributes less. Tempo learns from recent onsets and can take a few beats to settle; it is an approximate motion cue, not a displayed BPM measurement. This uses the same FFT worker/windows and playback timestamps, with fixed-size history and no additional decoder. Seek/track resets clear rhythm state; sleep fade attenuates both bars and beat strength. A single display-frame smoothing loop replaces repeatedly restarted spectrum animations; the cat limit and physics step rate are unchanged.
- Grab a cat by its visible body, drag it with a slight jelly stretch, and release to toss it with capped momentum. Held cats can nudge their neighbors and stay inside the scene. Taps still bounce; keyboard/accessibility activation is unchanged. Gestures starting on empty space still scroll or swipe pages, and cancelled drags drop safely without a fling.
- The five original cats gather at the top center in a compact group (three above, two below), pause for 180ms, then fall together with varied downward speed and gentle rotation. Their silhouettes start separated; the existing gravity, jelly collisions and landing effects handle their descent without a second physics loop. The entrance runs once per Activity session, not on track changes, Playlist/Customize navigation, background/foreground, or rotation. A ViewModel retains cat bodies, splits, rotation, spectrum/peak levels and the entrance flag; a fresh Activity session starts five originals. The two pager pages stay composed, and hidden scenes freeze their clocks and retain their surfaces instead of clearing them. Interrupted entrances retain the current falling positions rather than restarting from the top. While paused, awake cats take occasional gentle sideways hops after settling; this pauses while sleeping, playing, or hidden. The physics loop sleeps between settled hops. The idle-hop timer is also gated directly by the resumed lifecycle, so it cannot mutate cat bodies while the Compose frame clock is stopped in the background; music playback continues.
- Double-tap an original Shiro to split it once into two smaller cats that pop apart. Children are about 71% of the original size and cannot split again: the five originals allow at most **10 active cats**. Their visible geometry, touch targets, and collision mass scale together. Single taps now bounce immediately on release, without waiting for the double-tap timeout. A second tap can follow the moving cat or land near its previous tap position within the system double-tap window; a different cat under the finger takes priority. Dragging still tosses and cancels a split gesture. Double-tapping a child bounces it. Only originals offer the accessibility action **Split into two smaller Shiros**. Splits persist through Playlist/Customize, background/foreground and configuration changes; a fresh app session starts with five originals.
- Drag two children together to merge them into one original-size cat, including children from different originals. Ordinary collisions do not merge, and original-size cats cannot grow further. The merged cat stays under your finger; only one merge happens per drag. Release and drag again to merge another pair. Merged originals can split again, keeping total body area and the **5–10 population bounds**.
- Each cat caches its exact deformed collision outline and reuses its mesh buffer. Translation alone, repeated boundary checks, and unchanged shapes no longer rebuild the mesh. All 96 outline points, the full 20×16 jelly mesh, physics substeps, spectrum bars, and visual effects remain intact. Automated comparisons check identical frame-by-frame physics before and after optimization; desktop timing is written to `app/build/reports/shiro-physics-performance.txt` and is not a device FPS measurement.
- Crowded-scene profiling also identified expensive boxed range checks in bar contact calculations. These now use primitive bounds and one reusable wave accumulator per cat. Fixed mesh coordinates and radii are precomputed per size; physics and drawing reuse mesh buffers, including when only rotation changes. Outlines keep the same 96 points in a direct list. Regression tests compare the previous and optimized calculations exactly, including crowded scenes; physics rate, mesh detail, and effects remain intact. The later one-generation change separately caps the player at ten cats.
- A later trace of the stalled 20-cat pile isolated cat-to-cat collisions as the main UI-thread cost. Collision checks now reject separated edges without allocating temporary points, test containment once per contour, and cache a convex envelope for the separating push. The full 96-point concave outline still decides contact, including empty dents; the envelope is not the hitbox. Tests compare 4,000 deformed/rotated pairs with the previous detector and verify separation. A dense 20-cat CPU comparison is saved to `app/build/reports/shiro-collision-performance.txt`; it includes cache construction and does not establish device FPS. Physics now carries fractional 120 Hz steps across display frames, avoiding an accidental extra substep from rounding upward, with bounded catch-up after stalls.
- Play/resume now eases the first audible spectrum rise over 450ms instead of immediately applying the fast-tempo attack. Silence/buffering does not consume that ramp, quick pause/resume retains existing bar heights, and returning to the scene rearms it. Normal beat/tempo response returns afterward. Display smoothing caps catch-up to the same 33ms budget as physics. Collision separation also walks the ordered convex perimeter for projection extremes instead of scanning every vertex for every axis; tests compare those extremes exactly against full scans without simplifying the concave contact shape.
- Peaks appear as translucent bars behind the live spectrum, extending from the same baseline to the held peak height with matching widths and artwork colors. They hold for 400ms, then fall at 18% of the spectrum height per second. The floating caps, outlines, and gaps above live bars are removed. Peak trails are decorative, fade near zero, and stop updating once a paused spectrum settles or the scene is hidden.
- Shiro landings on visible bars create a small expanding ripple and four tiny droplets in the artwork color. Impact speed controls their size; resting contacts and the empty baseline do not splash. Effects fade within roughly half a second, have a per-cat cooldown and a fixed count limit, and freeze with the scene while hidden. They share the existing physics loop.
- Covers crossfade over 600ms when the next artwork load finishes, including transitions to tracks without art. The previous cover stays during loading; cancelled older loads cannot replace the latest selection. Song titles are 18sp and artists 14sp, both allowing two lines, with slightly larger composer text and spacing.
- An active sleep timer softens the lighting. During its fade, the curve and lighting settle with the audio. After timer expiry the scene stays dim until playback resumes or the timer is changed/cancelled.
- Spectrum analysis only runs while the main scene is in the foreground. Background audio remains owned by the playback service. No new library or rendering engine is required.
- Audio visualization uses only the live decoded PCM from the playback pipeline, including frequency bands, relative loudness, and rhythm/tempo response. Offline whole-song audio mapping, playlist map preparation, cached-map status dots, and the Delete Map Cache action are removed. Customize retains eye settings and the manual tap preview, without map-driven beat squishes. Artwork decoding and palette extraction remain off the UI thread.
- The spectrum reflects the source audio and the app's fade, not device speaker volume. Encoded passthrough routes without decoded PCM remain playable but have a flat spectrum. On scene return, missing PCM is distinct from actual silence: the displayed wave holds for up to one second while fresh audible frames arrive, without stopping visible cat physics or liquid motion. Valid silent frames and Pause still descend normally. Unsupported/no-PCM routes fade flat after the bounded handoff rather than retaining stale audio forever.

## Playback additions

- **Menu → Add home widget** opens the launcher's pin prompt for **Shiro player**. It is also available from the home-screen widget picker. The resizable widget shows artwork, title/artist, previous/play/pause/next controls, and an awake or resting Shiro. Tapping the artwork, song details, or cat opens the app. Its controls use the existing playback session, including restoration, audio focus, unavailable-track recovery and sleep timers; there is no second player. Empty playlists link to the app, buffering keeps Pause available, and connection errors offer an Open Shiro message. Modern launchers receive artwork-based surface colors; older Android versions keep the pale blue surface with artwork-colored text/controls.
- Widget changes follow playback events rather than a continuous animation or polling timer. A single cover thumbnail (at most 256 pixels per side) and two cached mascot poses keep image updates small. The widget uses embedded artwork already supplied by the player; it never starts another audio decoder or cat simulation. One atomic cache stores the last cover and its track identity, and restored metadata starts paused. Full widget image updates replace old bitmap references. The system media notification/card remains separate and retains Android's standard controls. See the [Android widget update guidance](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/appwidgets/advanced).
- Menu rows inside the opaque drawer use a simple tinted surface and one thin border. They no longer stack another glass reflection and elevation layer behind each label. The main player's glass surfaces and pressed feedback remain.
- Playback and the playlist live in an Android media service, with notification and lock-screen controls. Music can continue when the screen is off or the app is swiped away while playing.
- Shiro blinks every 3–7 seconds while idle and the app is visible.
- Open the menu and tap **Sleep timer** for 15, 30, or 60 minutes, **End of track**, or **Cancel timer**. Timed sleep counts elapsed time, including pauses. End of track follows the new song if you skip. Timer expiry pauses music and clears the timer.
- Open an active minute timer and tap **+5 minutes** to extend its existing deadline. It works while paused and restores normal volume during a fade without resuming music. This option is available only for minute timers; it does not change End of track into a countdown or revive an expired timer.
- Music fades over the final 30 seconds of timed sleep or a track-end timer. Cancelling/changing the timer restores normal playback volume, and expiry restores it after pausing so the next play starts normally. Track-end fading follows seeking/skipping; tracks with unknown duration still pause at the end but cannot fade in advance. Device volume is unchanged.
- Starting End of track with fewer than 30 seconds left fades from the current volume over the remaining time, without an initial volume drop. Seeking within the fade starts a new slope from the current volume; seeking beyond the final 30 seconds restores normal volume.
- Folder scans update the playlist only after every selected folder finishes successfully. Unavailable folders, incomplete provider results, or scan errors retain existing tracks from the selected folders and show Retry. Explicit folder removals prune their tracks immediately, even if another selected folder is unavailable. Scanning stays inside the selected document tree, with no device-wide MediaStore fallback. Only exact URI matches with unchanged file size, modification time, and filename reuse tag metadata; unknown fingerprints are re-read. Four reusable metadata readers handle new/changed files after discovery succeeds, and a scan lock prevents overlapping reader pools during rapid reloads. Playlist JSON writes run off the UI thread. New reloads or folder changes cancel older scans; stale results cannot update the queue or cache. Removing all folders clears the queue immediately.
- Playlist saves use an atomic file replacement and recover the last completed save after an interrupted write. Failed saves show **Retry save**, while the current playlist remains usable. Restoring a saved playlist filters out folders that were explicitly removed, even if saving their removal failed.
- The play/pause button stays on Pause during loading or temporary playback suppression, so a tap cancels pending playback. A loading label distinguishes buffering from a paused song; dancing still follows actual audio playback.
- Failed tracks are marked **Unavailable** in Playlist. While playback is requested, the service tries another track in queue/shuffle order, bypassing repeat-one for the failure. If every candidate fails, it pauses and cancels sleep instead of retrying forever. Pause, a queue change, or a manual track selection cancels a pending automatic skip. Timed sleep expiry wins over recovery; an End of track timer pauses and clears if its track fails. Tap an unavailable row to retry after fixing its file or folder access. Unavailable markers last for the service session and clear when a track plays successfully.
- Tap the heart beside the current song or a playlist row to save/remove a favorite. **All tracks / Favorites** filters the playlist and works with search. Favorites are saved by full track URI and survive restarts and temporarily removing/readding a folder. The filter changes the displayed list, not the playback queue; next/automatic advancement still use all tracks.
- While a sleep timer is active, Shiro's eyes gently narrow on the main and Customize screens, including while dancing. Blinking and tap reactions still work; cancelling or finishing the timer clears the sleepy expression.
- Timers last for the current playback service session; they do not survive force-stop, reboot, or process termination. Playback position is saved periodically and on player changes; reopening after a process restart restores paused playback.

## Manual checks in Android Studio

These are test steps, not recorded passing results. Sync Gradle and run the `app` configuration on an Android device or emulator. Add a folder with at least three short audio tracks.

1. Play a song, press Home, then lock the screen. Check that audio continues and notification/lock-screen play, pause, previous, and next controls work. Let a song finish to check automatic advancement in the background.
2. Enable shuffle, then repeat-one, and check each in the background. Reopen the app and rotate the device: the song, position, and toggle states should remain in sync. Swipe away the app while playing, then reopen it.
3. Disconnect wired/Bluetooth headphones while playing: playback should pause. Check another app taking audio focus, then return to Shiro.
4. Pause music and watch Shiro for 10 seconds. Check blinking on the main and Customize screens; starting music should end idle blinking.
5. Set a 15-minute timer, lock the screen, and verify music pauses at expiry. Reopen before expiry to check the countdown. Test changing and cancelling a timer, and pausing/resuming while it counts down.
6. For a quick sleep check, seek near a song's end, select **End of track**, and let it finish. Check that no next song starts, including with shuffle or repeat-one enabled. Resume afterward, then test skipping while End of track is set.
7. Add/reload a folder during playback: the current song should keep its position if it still exists. Remove its folder and check that playback pauses safely. Removing all folders should clear the queue and timer.
8. To check the fade quickly, seek to 35 seconds before the end and choose **End of track**. Listen for a gradual fade in the final 30 seconds, followed by a pause. Resume and confirm normal volume. Repeat with the screen locked, and with repeat-one enabled.
9. During a fade, cancel the timer or change it to 15 minutes: normal volume should return immediately. With End of track still active, seek backward or skip to a longer track and check that the fade resets. Pause/resume mid-fade and verify it follows the remaining track time. Also check the last 30 seconds of an actual minute timer while the screen is locked.
10. Enable a timer and inspect the sleepy eyes on the main and customization screens. Pause to check blinking and tap the character. Reopen/rotate while a timer is active. Cancel it to restore the usual expression; after expiry Shiro stays sleepy until playback resumes or **Restore lighting** is selected in the sleep dialog.
11. Make a selected folder unavailable (for example, disconnect removable storage) and tap Reload. Verify a retry message appears and the existing playlist and saved position remain. Reconnect storage and Retry. Also test a genuinely empty, accessible folder: a successful scan should remove its old tracks.
12. With a large folder, start a reload and then quickly add/remove folders or reload again. Only the final folder selection should appear. Remove all folders while a scan is running and wait: old tracks must not reappear. Rotate during scanning, then reopen the app to check the cached list. Compare first and second reloads with thousands of files; repeat scans should avoid reopening unchanged files for tag extraction. Include duplicate filenames in different folders, edit a file, disconnect a subfolder, and test a provider without file timestamps. Existing tracks must survive incomplete listings and unrelated folders must never be imported.
13. Seek to 10 seconds before the end, enable End of track, and confirm there is no immediate volume drop: music should fade over those 10 seconds. Repeat with 5 seconds left, and seek forward/backward during a fade. Confirm cancellation restores volume and expiry pauses before restoring it.
14. Start a track that loads slowly and tap Pause while **Loading…** is shown. Wait for loading to finish: playback must remain paused. Resume, then check that a failed track still offers Play for retry.
15. Add folders A and B, make B unavailable, then remove A from Inputs. A's tracks should disappear immediately even when refreshing B fails. Check with A currently playing (playback pauses), with a retained track playing (position preserved), and after reopening the app. Test a nested folder to confirm all its scanned descendants are removed.
16. To inspect interrupted-save recovery, use a disposable emulator: after a successful save, interrupt a later save or simulate a write failure with the debugger. Reopen and confirm the previous completed cache can be read. A failed write should show Retry save; retry after restoring storage access. No corrupt partial playlist should replace the completed save.
17. Start a 15-minute timer, wait briefly, and tap **+5 minutes**: remaining time should increase by five minutes, not reset to five. Repeat twice, including while paused and during the final fade. The pause state must remain unchanged and volume should recover. Check that End of track has no extension button and that an expired timer cannot be extended.
18. Use disposable test files: load a few valid tracks, then delete one without reloading, or include a corrupt audio file. Play the unavailable track and verify recovery reaches a valid track, with an Unavailable row marker and no blocking error dialog. Repeat with the screen locked, shuffle on, and repeat-one on. Do not use your only copy of any music for this check.
19. Use a queue where every track is unavailable, including a one-track queue. It should stop after trying the candidates, without an endless loop. Press Pause during recovery and wait: no new track should start. Select a different track during recovery and confirm the pending skip does not override it. Restore a file and tap its row to retry; its marker should clear when playback succeeds.
20. Check failures with sleep enabled: End of track should stop on a failed song; an expired minute timer must prevent recovery from starting another song. With an unexpired minute timer, recovery should keep the existing countdown. If no playable candidates remain, the timer should be cleared.
21. Favorite songs from both screens, search within Favorites, and remove the last favorite while filtering. Favorites rows use a glass X button to remove a favorite; All tracks and the player retain their hearts. Verify icons, empty states, and selected-song highlighting agree. Tapping X or a heart must not start or change playback, and removing the last favorite should show the empty state. Reopen/rotate and check persistence. Use duplicate filenames in different folders to verify only the selected URI is favorited. Remove and readd the same folder and verify its saved hearts return.
22. Add **Shiro player** from the menu or launcher widget picker. Check play/pause/previous/next against the app and notification, both while the app is open and after swiping it away. Paused skips must stay paused; Pause during buffering must cancel pending playback. Test a stopped service, an empty playlist, unavailable files, sleep expiry, colorful/monochrome/missing artwork, long metadata, large fonts, resizing, and two widget instances. The cat should change pose on audible playback changes; it is not a continuously animated spectrum. Reboot and confirm the restored widget does not claim music is playing. Tapping artwork/details/cat should open the same player. Check actual launcher appearance on your phone; the build and unit tests do not establish launcher rendering.
23. Open the drawer and Customize, then inspect menu rows and the Eye Color card without pressing anything. Rows should have an even tinted fill, no rectangular white inner shine behind the labels, and retain a brief response on press. If text backgrounds still appear, compare the phone's accessibility text-contrast setting; Android can add its own text outlines/backgrounds independently of Shiro's card styling.
