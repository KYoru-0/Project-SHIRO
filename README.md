# Project-SHIRO
Shiro is a round fluff that listens to your music, vibe to it while being absolutely adorable on your Android phone

## Playback additions

- Playback and the playlist live in an Android media service, with notification and lock-screen controls. Music can continue when the screen is off or the app is swiped away while playing.
- Shiro blinks every 3–7 seconds while idle and the app is visible.
- Tap **Sleep timer** at the top right for 15, 30, or 60 minutes, **End of track**, or **Cancel timer**. Timed sleep counts elapsed time, including pauses. End of track follows the new song if you skip. Timer expiry pauses music and clears the timer.
- Open an active minute timer and tap **+5 minutes** to extend its existing deadline. It works while paused and restores normal volume during a fade without resuming music. This option is available only for minute timers; it does not change End of track into a countdown or revive an expired timer.
- Music fades over the final 30 seconds of timed sleep or a track-end timer. Cancelling/changing the timer restores normal playback volume, and expiry restores it after pausing so the next play starts normally. Track-end fading follows seeking/skipping; tracks with unknown duration still pause at the end but cannot fade in advance. Device volume is unchanged.
- Starting End of track with fewer than 30 seconds left fades from the current volume over the remaining time, without an initial volume drop. Seeking within the fade starts a new slope from the current volume; seeking beyond the final 30 seconds restores normal volume.
- Folder scans update the playlist only after every selected folder finishes successfully. Unavailable folders, incomplete provider results, or scan errors retain existing tracks from the selected folders and show Retry. Explicit folder removals prune their tracks immediately, even if another selected folder is unavailable. New reloads or folder changes cancel older scans; stale results cannot update the queue or cache. Removing all folders clears the queue immediately.
- Playlist saves use an atomic file replacement and recover the last completed save after an interrupted write. Failed saves show **Retry save**, while the current playlist remains usable. Restoring a saved playlist filters out folders that were explicitly removed, even if saving their removal failed.
- The play/pause button stays on Pause during loading or temporary playback suppression, so a tap cancels pending playback. A loading label distinguishes buffering from a paused song; dancing still follows actual audio playback.
- Failed tracks are marked **Unavailable** in Playlist. While playback is requested, the service tries another track in queue/shuffle order, bypassing repeat-one for the failure. If every candidate fails, it pauses and cancels sleep instead of retrying forever. Pause, a queue change, or a manual track selection cancels a pending automatic skip. Timed sleep expiry wins over recovery; an End of track timer pauses and clears if its track fails. Tap an unavailable row to retry after fixing its file or folder access. Unavailable markers last for the service session and clear when a track plays successfully.
- Tap the heart beside the current song or a playlist row to save/remove a favorite. **All tracks / Favorites** filters the playlist and works with search. Favorites are saved by full track URI and survive restarts and temporarily removing/readding a folder. The filter changes the displayed list, not the playback queue; next/automatic advancement still use all tracks.
- While a sleep timer is active, Shiro's eyes gently narrow on the main and Customize screens, including while dancing. Blinking and tap reactions still work; cancelling or finishing the timer clears the sleepy expression.
- Timers last for the current playback service session; they do not survive force-stop, reboot, or process termination. Playback position is saved periodically and on player changes; reopening after a process restart restores paused playback.

## Manual checks in Android Studio

These are test steps, not recorded passing results. Sync Gradle and run the `app` configuration on an Android device or emulator. Add a folder with at least three short audio tracks.

1. Play a song, press Home, then lock the screen. Check that audio continues and notification/lock-screen play, pause, previous, and next controls work. Let a song finish to check automatic advancement in the background.
2. Enable shuffle, then repeat-one, and check each in the background. Reopen the app and rotate the device: the song, position, and toggle states should remain in sync. Swipe away the app while playing, then reopen it.
3. Disconnect wired/Bluetooth headphones while playing: playback should pause. Check another app taking audio focus, then return to Shiro.
4. Pause music and watch Shiro for 10 seconds. Check blinking on the main and Customize screens; starting music should end idle blinking.
5. Set a 15-minute timer, lock the screen, and verify music pauses at expiry. Reopen before expiry to check the countdown. Test changing and cancelling a timer, and pausing/resuming while it counts down.
6. For a quick sleep check, seek near a song's end, select **End of track**, and let it finish. Check that no next song starts, including with shuffle or repeat-one enabled. Resume afterward, then test skipping while End of track is set.
7. Add/reload a folder during playback: the current song should keep its position if it still exists. Remove its folder and check that playback pauses safely. Removing all folders should clear the queue and timer.
8. To check the fade quickly, seek to 35 seconds before the end and choose **End of track**. Listen for a gradual fade in the final 30 seconds, followed by a pause. Resume and confirm normal volume. Repeat with the screen locked, and with repeat-one enabled.
9. During a fade, cancel the timer or change it to 15 minutes: normal volume should return immediately. With End of track still active, seek backward or skip to a longer track and check that the fade resets. Pause/resume mid-fade and verify it follows the remaining track time. Also check the last 30 seconds of an actual minute timer while the screen is locked.
10. Enable a timer and inspect the sleepy eyes on both screens, with dance off, Sway, and Squish. Pause to check blinking and tap the character. Reopen/rotate while a timer is active, then cancel it or let it finish: the eyes should return to their usual expression.
11. Make a selected folder unavailable (for example, disconnect removable storage) and tap Reload. Verify a retry message appears and the existing playlist and saved position remain. Reconnect storage and Retry. Also test a genuinely empty, accessible folder: a successful scan should remove its old tracks.
12. With a large folder, start a reload and then quickly add/remove folders or reload again. Only the final folder selection should appear. Remove all folders while a scan is running and wait: old tracks must not reappear. Rotate during scanning, then reopen the app to check the cached list.
13. Seek to 10 seconds before the end, enable End of track, and confirm there is no immediate volume drop: music should fade over those 10 seconds. Repeat with 5 seconds left, and seek forward/backward during a fade. Confirm cancellation restores volume and expiry pauses before restoring it.
14. Start a track that loads slowly and tap Pause while **Loading…** is shown. Wait for loading to finish: playback must remain paused. Resume, then check that a failed track still offers Play for retry.
15. Add folders A and B, make B unavailable, then remove A from Inputs. A's tracks should disappear immediately even when refreshing B fails. Check with A currently playing (playback pauses), with a retained track playing (position preserved), and after reopening the app. Test a nested folder to confirm all its scanned descendants are removed.
16. To inspect interrupted-save recovery, use a disposable emulator: after a successful save, interrupt a later save or simulate a write failure with the debugger. Reopen and confirm the previous completed cache can be read. A failed write should show Retry save; retry after restoring storage access. No corrupt partial playlist should replace the completed save.
17. Start a 15-minute timer, wait briefly, and tap **+5 minutes**: remaining time should increase by five minutes, not reset to five. Repeat twice, including while paused and during the final fade. The pause state must remain unchanged and volume should recover. Check that End of track has no extension button and that an expired timer cannot be extended.
18. Use disposable test files: load a few valid tracks, then delete one without reloading, or include a corrupt audio file. Play the unavailable track and verify recovery reaches a valid track, with an Unavailable row marker and no blocking error dialog. Repeat with the screen locked, shuffle on, and repeat-one on. Do not use your only copy of any music for this check.
19. Use a queue where every track is unavailable, including a one-track queue. It should stop after trying the candidates, without an endless loop. Press Pause during recovery and wait: no new track should start. Select a different track during recovery and confirm the pending skip does not override it. Restore a file and tap its row to retry; its marker should clear when playback succeeds.
20. Check failures with sleep enabled: End of track should stop on a failed song; an expired minute timer must prevent recovery from starting another song. With an unexpired minute timer, recovery should keep the existing countdown. If no playable candidates remain, the timer should be cleared.
21. Favorite songs from both screens, search within Favorites, and remove the last favorite while filtering. Verify hearts, empty states, and selected-song highlighting agree. Tapping a heart must not start or change playback. Reopen/rotate and check persistence. Use duplicate filenames in different folders to verify only the selected URI is favorited. Remove and readd the same folder and verify its saved hearts return.
